# "Caring Loudly" — Personal Reels Series Plan

A recurring short-form video series for Wynnona's personal Instagram.
Format reference: candid daily-life video + confessional text overlay + signature caption.

---

## Why the reference format works (so we can own it, not copy it)

1. **The gap is the story.** The footage is mundane (just walking); the text is intimate. Your brain fills the space between them with a person, a relationship, a gift. Zero production, maximum imagination.
2. **Second person = casting call.** "You said you like it" lets every viewer cast their own person as the "you." That's why it gets shared: sending the reel to someone IS the gesture. The reel is a sendable object, not just content.
3. **Present tense = caught in the act.** "I'm secretly on my way" feels like being let in on a secret in real time, not a statement about the past.
4. **Low production = believability.** If it looked staged, the sincerity would collapse. The casualness is the proof.

Our version keeps those four mechanics but changes the voice: more specific, more personal, and anchored to one thesis — the quiet rebellion against nonchalance.

---

## Core creative concept

A recurring series of candid, unproduced Reels where one ordinary moment of my day — walking, wrapping, writing, driving — is paired with a single small, specific confession about how I love people: what I remembered, what I noticed, what I'm secretly on my way to do. Each overlay reads like a thought caught mid-action, revealing that behind my most "random" errands there is always someone I care about. Together, the series is a quiet rebellion against nonchalance: proof that remembering the little things and loving people out loud is the least embarrassing thing a person can do.

One-line north star: **"In a world where everyone is trying to be nonchalant, I still want to care loudly."**

## Making it recognizably *Wynnona* over time

### 1. A writing formula (the real signature)
Every overlay follows the same two-beat structure:
- **Beat 1 — the tiny detail:** what they said, did, or mentioned ("You mentioned it once, three months ago…")
- **Beat 2 — the disproportionate response:** what I did about it ("…It's in my bag now.")

Rules: always lowercase-casual, always present tense or just-happened, always *specific* (three months, aisle 4, week 4, 2am — real numbers and details make it feel lived, not written). Never explain the feeling; show the action.

### 2. One font, one placement, forever
Choose a single Instagram text style (clean classic sans, or the typewriter font to echo the letter motif) in white or cream, always in the same position (center or lower third), always lowercase. After ~5 posts, the type alone is recognizable in the feed.

### 3. A visual language
- **Hands as the recurring motif:** wrapping, writing, carrying, pouring, holding. Love as something hands do.
- Natural light only — golden hour and soft daylight. Movement in every clip (walking, driving, doing).
- **A red thread:** one recurring color accent — red ribbon on every gift, red ink in every letter, a red heart sticker sealing every envelope. Quiet, ownable, and it nods to the red thread of fate. People will start noticing it before they can name it.

### 4. The letter signature
- Caption always signs off **"love, wynnona"**.
- Your own first comment on every post is a **"p.s."** — e.g. "p.s. check on your soft friends," "p.s. tell them today," "p.s. yes, this one's about you." The p.s. becomes the thing people scroll to find.

### 5. Sound
Reuse 2–3 soft recurring audios rather than chasing trends. Sonic consistency = series recognition on mute-off.

### 6. Content architecture (so it never feels like a campaign)
- **~80% core format:** candid clip + overlay + signature caption. One clean thought per reel.
- **~20% "receipts":** occasionally show the thing itself — the finished letter, the wrapped gift, the "reminded me of you" photo album — proof the words are real.
- **Rare manifesto posts (every 10–15 reels):** state the thesis plainly once in a while — "in a world where everyone is trying to be nonchalant, I still want to care loudly" — over the most ordinary footage you have. These become the pinned posts.

### 7. Cadence
1–2 reels per week, ideally a fixed day, batch-filmed from real errands (you're already doing the actions; just film 10 seconds of them).

### 8. Zero-time filming plan (one 10-minute shoot = a month of content)
Do NOT reuse the reference creator's video — it's his copyrighted footage and his face, and the series only works if the person on screen is you.

Instead, film once during an errand you're already doing:
1. **60s** — front camera at 0.5x, held low, just walking. Don't perform; look around, never at the lens.
2. **30s** — walking while holding your coffee or tote.
3. **20s** — close on hands: pushing a door, pressing a crosswalk button, picking something off a shelf.
4. **30s** — sitting in a café window or bus seat, looking out.

Reusing the same clip across many reels is fine (no Instagram penalty) and strategically smart — the same walk/jacket/street becomes the recognizable wrapper while only the text changes. Keep it fresh by trimming a different 8–10 seconds each time, occasionally flipping horizontally or adding a slow zoom, and rotating the 2–3 recurring audios. Existing camera-roll clips (car windows, cooking, walking) work as-is with an overlay.
