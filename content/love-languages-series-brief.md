# Love Languages Video Series — Creative Brief

*Prepared for Wynnona · July 2026 · Non-talking, visual-first short-form series*

---

## Part 1: Three Creative Directions

### Direction A — "Proof of Love" (The Museum Angle)

**Concept.** Love treated as physical evidence. Every episode is a museum exhibit: one ordinary object or micro-ritual, filmed like an artifact, that proves somebody loved somebody. The thesis of the series: *love leaves evidence — you just have to know what to look for.* It never mentions "the 5 love languages" by name; it archives the real, hyper-specific dialects people actually use (cut fruit, warmed cars, saved voicemails).

**Visual identity.** Museum meets home movie. Macro shots of hands and objects, tungsten kitchen light at night, film grain and slight halation, kraft-paper tones. No faces (hands, backs of heads, silhouettes only — anonymity makes it universal; every viewer casts their own people). Typography: one serif/typewriter face, lowercase. The signature asset is the **end-card label** — a museum placard with an exhibit number, a title, a poetic "materials" list, and a donor credit.

**Repeatable format (30–45s vertical).**
1. Cold open on the object mid-action + one declarative hook line
2. 3–5 tactile shots of the ritual (foley loud, music quiet)
3. "The Turn" — one line that reframes what we watched (music enters here)
4. The Label — the placard end card, held 3+ seconds (the save/screenshot target)
5. The Door — CTA line + the same closing sound every episode (sonic logo)

**Example episodes.**
- *Exhibit №001 — The Cut Fruit.* "my mother has never said sorry in her life." Knife, orange, the good plate slid onto a desk. Turn: "she says it like this."
- *Exhibit №007 — The Saved Voicemail.* Storage-full warning; thumb hovers over a 34-second voicemail from "Grandma ❤️", dated three years ago. "delete?" → "never."
- *Exhibit №008 — The Last Bite.* One dessert, two spoons, portions getting suspiciously uneven. "how to split dessert with someone you love: badly, on purpose."

### Direction B — "The Unspoken Dictionary" (The Linguistics Angle)

**Concept.** If everyone has a love language, then every family, couple, and friendship speaks an untranslatable dialect — so the series invents the missing vocabulary. Each episode coins one word for a specific way of loving, presented as a dictionary entry, then proves the word exists with cinematic footage. (Same emotional engine as *The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows*, but for care instead of melancholy.)

**Visual identity.** Editorial and typographic: paper texture, letterpress-style serif entries, muted film tones. The word gets typed on screen character by character (typewriter sound), then the footage plays *under* the definition like a living illustration.

**Repeatable format (30–40s).**
1. The word appears + phonetic spelling ("door-light, *n.*")
2. The definition, one line at a time
3. Three shots of visual proof
4. "Used in a sentence" — the emotional gut-punch line
5. End card: the full entry as a shareable dictionary page

**Example episodes.**
- *doorlight (n.)* — the porch light left on until everyone is home; a lighthouse operated by someone who loves you.
- *echo-texting (v.)* — sending someone things that remind you of them; the act of saying "you exist in my head when you're not here."
- *platespeak (n.)* — apologies served as food; fluency common among parents who cannot pronounce "I'm sorry."

---

### Direction C — "Shot on Love" (The Home-Video Memory Angle)

**Concept.** Every episode is a memory filmed from the point of view of the person being loved — the camera is your eyes. Super-8 / camcorder texture, date stamps, the slightly-wrong framing of real home video. The thesis: *some moments were never filmed, but you remember them in footage quality.* It weaponizes nostalgia directly.

**Visual identity.** VHS/Super-8 grain, 4:3 frame letterboxed inside vertical, camcorder date stamps (REC ● 1998-ish), warm degraded color, tape hiss under the music. Text appears as camcorder OSD.

**Repeatable format (30–45s).**
1. Black + tape-load sound + "MEMORY №__" and a date stamp
2. POV footage of a small act of love happening *to you*
3. One line of text where the timestamp would be
4. Freeze-frame ending + rewind sound
5. Recurring closing card: "some cameras never existed. you just remember."

**Example episodes.**
- *Memory №03 — The Drive Home.* POV from the backseat as a kid: streetlights strobing past, a parent's eyes in the rearview checking on you, then being carried inside half-asleep. "you pretended to sleep so they'd carry you. they knew. they carried you anyway."
- *Memory №07 — How Your Friends See You.* POV of a friend filming you laughing badly at your own joke. "this is what they see when you think you're being embarrassing."
- *Memory №11 — The Wave.* Grandparents on the porch, waving, getting smaller through the rear window — and not going inside until the car turns the corner. "they wave until you're gone. every time."

## Part 2: Recommendation

**Direction A — "Proof of Love" — is the strongest, and it can absorb the other two later.**

1. **It's the most production-friendly for a non-talking, one-person operation.** Objects and hands, no cast, no location scouting — a kitchen at night and a macro lens. Consistency is what makes a signature series, and this is the format you can execute 52 times a year without burnout.
2. **The numbered-exhibit device creates collectibility.** Numbers make episodes citable ("I'm exhibit 4," "tag your exhibit 6 person"), turn the catalog into a binge, and make the 100th episode a milestone event.
3. **The interaction loop is structural, not bolted on.** "Donate an exhibit to the museum" turns the comment section into your writers' room, and crediting donors on future labels ("Donor: @username") gives people a reason to comment that no generic CTA can match.
4. **It's extensible.** Direction B becomes labels' vocabulary (coined words can appear on placards, or as a "Language Wing" sub-series). Direction C becomes the museum's "moving image acquisitions." You're choosing a universe, not just a format.

## Part 3: The Developed Concept

### Series title

**PROOF OF LOVE** — *a museum of the ways we care*

Each episode: **Exhibit №___ — [Title]**. Hashtag: #ProofOfLove. Drop cadence: "the museum opens every Sunday."

### The unique angle (why it isn't generic love-languages content)

Generic love-languages content *explains categories*. This series *presents evidence* — hyper-specific, sensory, culturally textured artifacts — and lets viewers do the emotional math themselves. It never says "acts of service"; it shows a father oiling a bike chain. The specificity is the originality: the more particular the object, the more universal the recognition.

### Storytelling style & craft rules

- 30–45 seconds, vertical, built for Reels/TikTok; label cards repurposed as IG carousels.
- **No faces.** Hands, objects, silhouettes, backs of heads. Viewers cast their own people.
- **Foley over music.** Knife on board, car door thunk, tape hiss. Music enters only at The Turn.
- **Declarative hooks, never questions.** "my mother has never said sorry in her life" out-hooks "what's your love language?"
- One typeface, lowercase, warm tungsten grade, film grain.
- Identical closing sound every episode (music-box note or tape-stop) — a sonic logo.

### The repeatable 5-beat template ("the museum protocol")

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Hook | 0–2s | Object mid-action + one declarative line |
| 2. The Ritual | 2–15s | 3–5 tactile shots of the act, foley-forward |
| 3. The Turn | 15–25s | One line that reframes everything; music enters |
| 4. The Label | 25–35s | Placard end card: EXHIBIT №, title, materials, donor. Hold 3s+ |
| 5. The Door | last 3s | CTA line + sonic logo |

**Label anatomy** (the poetry lives in "Materials"):

> EXHIBIT №001 — THE CUT FRUIT
> Materials: one orange, pride, tenderness
> Donor: every child who understood
> On loan from: the kitchen, 9:40 p.m.

### The first 10 episodes

**№001 — The Cut Fruit** *(family · apology as service)*
Hook: macro knife halving an orange; "my mother has never said sorry in her life."
Shots: peeling; arranging on the good plate; plate slid onto a desk next to homework; door pulled almost-closed; a hand taking a slice.
Turn: "she says it like this."
Label materials: one orange, pride, tenderness.
Door: "what did 'sorry' look like in your house?"

**№002 — The Warm Car** *(romantic · quiet acts)*
Hook: frost melting off a windshield from the inside at dawn; "he has never once said it first."
Shots: keys lifted in the dark; breath fog; defroster hum; passenger door opened from inside; coffee in the cupholder, label turned outward.
Turn: "but the car is always warm."
Label materials: 6:45 a.m., one engine, patience.
Door: "tag someone who loves in degrees."

**№003 — The Screenshot Folder** *(friendship · being remembered)*
Hook: thumb scrolling a photo album titled with a friend's name; "she keeps a folder with your name on it."
Shots: memes; a dress captioned "this is so you"; a dog that looks like yours; typing "THIS IS US."
Turn: "being remembered is a love language."
Label materials: 312 screenshots, no occasion.
Door: "send this to the person whose folder you're in."

**№004 — The Containers** *(family · feeding as love)*
Hook: butter container opened → frozen soup inside; "nothing in this house is what it says it is."
Shots: towers of yogurt tubs and ice-cream tubs full of stew; sharpie dates; a bag pressed into hands at the door; the bag riding shotgun home.
Turn: "she never lets you leave empty-handed."
Label materials: one cookie tin (contains sewing supplies), one butter tub (contains dinner), abundance.
Door: "what's inside your family's containers?"

**№005 — Evidence I Am Learning** *(self-love)*
Hook: a bed being made in morning light; "someone has been taking care of me lately."
Shots: a glass of water set out the night before; vitamins by the kettle; clothes laid out; a note on the mirror: "you did enough today"; a table set for one, properly.
Turn: "it's me. i'm someone."
Label: EXHIBIT №005 — EVIDENCE I AM LEARNING. Materials: one glass of water, eight hours, forgiveness. Donor: me, for me.
Door: "save this as a receipt for later."

**№006 — Home ✓** *(friendship · text me when you're home)*
Hook: night street, one walker; a phone screen reading "text me when you're home"; "the oldest promise in friendship."
Shots: bus window; keys in a door; a lamp clicking on; typing "home"; the typing dots on the other side appear instantly — they were waiting.
Turn: "someone's night doesn't end until yours does."
Label materials: one porch light, 11:47 p.m., relief.
Door: "tag your 'text me when you're home' person."

**№007 — The Saved Voicemail** *(family/grief · love that outlives)*
Hook: "Storage Almost Full" screen; thumb hovering over a voicemail from "Grandma ❤️", 0:34, dated three years ago; "delete?"
Shots: the waveform playing — we never hear it (silence makes it everyone's grandmother); phone pressed to an ear, eyes closed; the same window chair through changing seasons.
Turn: "never."
Label: Materials: 34 seconds, 3 years, everything. Status: permanent collection.
Door: no CTA — just "keep what you can't replay." (Grief episodes generate "this reminded me of someone" comments on their own.)

**№008 — The Last Bite** *(romantic · sacrifice in miniature)*
Hook: one dessert, two spoons; "how to split dessert with someone you love:"
Shots: alternating spoons; portions going suspiciously uneven; one spoon nudging the final piece across the plate; two hands pushing the plate at each other — the whole argument in gestures.
Turn: "badly. on purpose."
Label materials: one slice, two spoons, an argument nobody wants to win.
Door: "tag the person who always gives you the last bite."

**№009 — The Fixed Things** *(family · fathers and maintenance)*
Hook: hands tightening a wobbly chair leg; "my father is not a man of words."
Shots: bike chain oiled; screen protector applied perfectly; tires checked before your road trip; a hinge sprayed mid-squeak; his silhouette in garage light.
Turn: "but nothing in my life squeaks."
Label materials: WD-40, quiet, thirty years. Donor: anonymous (he'd hate the attention).
Door: "what has he fixed without being asked?"

**№010 — Donated by You** *(community · all loves)*
Hook: "one month ago, i opened a museum. you filled it."
Concept: 5–6 rapid-fire mini-exhibits recreated from real comments on episodes 1–9, each label crediting the commenter: "Donor: @___."
Ending label: EXHIBIT №∞ — THE COMMENT SECTION. Materials: strangers, proof.
Door: "the museum is open every sunday. donations welcome."
This episode announces the rule that powers the whole series: comments become canon.

### Interactivity systems

1. **The donation box.** Every Door beat invites viewers to "donate an exhibit." Best donations become episodes with on-label credit. Comment → canon → credit is the flywheel.
2. **Numbered collectibility.** "Which exhibit are you?" / "tag your №006 person." Numbers make the series citable and binge-able.
3. **Blank placard template.** Post an empty label ("EXHIBIT №___ — ________ / Materials: ________") as a story/carousel template people fill in and share.
4. **Save-bait labels.** The end card is designed to be screenshot as a quote card — hold it long enough to save.
5. **Wings.** Group the catalog into collections for profile browsing: the Family Wing, the Friendship Wing, the Self Wing, the Permanent Collection (grief). Wings also become themed weeks/months.
6. **Opening hours.** Fixed weekly drop ("the museum opens sundays") turns posting into ritual.

### Long-term repeatability

- The template is subject-agnostic: seasonal wings (Holiday Wing, Long-Distance Wing, First-Apartment Wing), milestone retrospectives ("100 exhibits"), audience-donated months.
- Directions B and C fold in as special collections: coined dictionary words on labels ("the Language Wing"), home-video POV pieces as "moving image acquisitions."
- Off-platform extensions when the catalog matures: printable placards, a zine ("the exhibition catalog"), and eventually a physical pop-up — a real museum night of donated objects, which is a PR story in itself.
